## The API is a separate subscription, not a Pro feature

This trips up more buyers than anything else on the page. DeepL's Pro plans are for people using the translator and writer. Getting DeepL into your own software, or into a CAT tool through a plug-in, runs on the API plans, which are priced on characters.

## What Pro adds over the free translator

The paid tiers buy four things the free tool does not give you.

**Text translation without a character ceiling.** DeepL advertises unlimited text translation on Translator Pro and unlimited writing suggestions on Write Pro, subject to the fair-use policy covered further down.

**Documents that keep their layout.** Pro handles more files and bigger files, and returns .docx, .pptx and .txt with fonts, images and formatting intact. Reviewers in translation and localisation consistently name this as the reason they pay.

**Glossaries.** You can build and maintain term lists so a brand name or a technical term comes out the same way every time. Note the tier effect: a June 2026 reviewer complained that the basic version allows exactly one glossary at no extra cost, which is fine for a single client and useless for a freelancer juggling five.

**Data that is not used for training.** This is the one that matters if you translate contracts, patents or internal product material. More on it below.

## The bill is rarely just the subscription

Two costs sit outside the plan price, and both appear in user complaints.

**Write Pro** is €6.99 per user per month on annual billing, VAT included, added on top of an existing DeepL subscription. A June 2026 reviewer also noted that integrating with common apps such as Word costs extra, so budget for the possibility that the workflow you want is not inside the tier you picked. If writing quality is the reason you are buying, price the add-on before comparing tiers.

**The API** is the second. Any plan that involves plugging DeepL into memoQ, Trados Studio, Across or your own product means an API subscription on top, not a bigger Pro plan.

## "Unlimited" comes with a fair-use clause

DeepL's pricing page carries a footnote that applies to every tier: a fair usage policy applies. That single line is the most important thing to understand before you buy, because "unlimited" in the marketing copy is not the same as "no limits in the contract." Third-party reviewers say the same thing from the other direction. GetApp's price-and-value summary notes that usage limits still exist even in paid plans, and one reviewer describes running into their plan's character limit when the product malfunctioned and they had to redo work.

In practice, ordinary professional use rarely hits a fair-use threshold. Bulk machine processing of large corpora on an individual plan will. If your use case looks like batch processing rather than human-in-the-loop translation, the API plans with published character blocks are the honest comparison, since you can see exactly what you are buying.

## Billing rules that decide whether you can change your mind later

DeepL's FAQ is unusually clear on this, and the details reward reading before you click buy.

- **Upgrades take effect immediately.** What you already paid is credited pro rata and deducted from the new plan.
- **Downgrades wait for the end of the cycle.** You keep the higher tier's features until the current period ends.
- **Adding seats is immediate and pro rata.** Removing a member is also immediate for billing, but that person keeps access until the cycle ends.
- **Cancellation is self-service.** Click cancel under Subscription in your account; it takes effect at the end of the current billing cycle and you keep Pro features until then.
- **No PayPal.** Cards (Visa, Mastercard, American Express, UnionPay, JCB, Discover, Diners Club, Cartes Bancaires) and SEPA Direct Debit on EUR accounts are accepted. Bank transfer by virtual IBAN exists for Team, Business and Enterprise annual subscriptions, only above a country-specific invoice threshold, in Japan and many European countries.
- **Volume discounts exist** for large seat counts and are handled case by case.

One team detail that is easy to miss: document translation limits can be shared two ways. Either the whole team draws from one pool with no per-person cap, or the administrator assigns an identical file allowance to every member. You set this in Settings. A five-person team where one person does all the document work should choose the shared pool; a team of equals may prefer the split.

DeepL states that Pro processing runs on ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II certified processes and infrastructure, in ISO 27001-certified data centres, and that it is fully compliant with GDPR and comparable regimes. For Pro subscribers, original content and processed results are stored only as long as technically needed to deliver the service and then deleted, and they are never used to train DeepL's models. If your legal or procurement team asks the question, that is the paragraph they want, and it is published rather than buried.

The same announcement points to a 30-day trial in newly opened markets; DeepL's pricing page only says a trial exists for Individual and Team plans, so treat 30 days as a promotional figure rather than a global entitlement.

## Who should skip Pro, and which tier to pick

**Stay on free** if you translate short passages, nothing confidential, and you do not need documents back in their original layout. The free allowance is described as generous by reviewers, and paying €8.99 to remove a limit you never hit is a waste.

**Pick Starter** if you are one person translating text plus a handful of decks or contracts a month, with one client's terminology to enforce. This is where most individual buyers land, and the €8.99 monthly price means you can stop without eating a year of commitment.

**Pick Advanced** if you are a working translator with several glossaries, a steady document load, and clients whose terminology conflicts. The December 2025 reviewer paying €30.00 a month was on this tier, and for full-time translation work the difference against Starter is small next to a single billable hour.

**Pick Ultimate** only if one seat is genuinely producing agency-level volume. Most people who think they need Ultimate need Advanced and a better filing system.

**Pick Team** from two seats upward, mainly for the shared quota and the admin controls rather than the translation itself. **Business** exists for the procurement side: annual terms and invoice payment. **Enterprise** is a contract negotiation.

**Skip Pro entirely and go to API Growth** if the goal is putting DeepL inside software, a CAT tool or an automated pipeline. No Pro tier substitutes for it.

## What reviewers actually complain about

Aggregate scores are high: 4.7 out of 5 from 156 reviews on Software Advice and 4.7 from 157 verified reviews on GetApp, with 87% positive on the latter and 96% of reviewers naming translation management as their use case. The complaints are specific and worth knowing about, because they cluster around service rather than output.

- **Support** (June 2026): a reviewer described reaching only a chatbot and FAQ pages, with emails to management going unanswered. A December 2025 reviewer reported a product fault, a reply that their issue "is not a priority," and a refusal of any compensation or goodwill gesture on a €30-a-month subscription.
- **Tier limits** (June 2026): one glossary on the basic version, paid integration for common apps like Word, American English as the default target, and having to reselect source and target languages on every visit.
- **Stability** (June 2025 and July 2025): a memory feature left broken for months, awkward copy-paste in the mobile app, and slowdowns at peak times. A stable internet connection is required throughout.

Do these change the recommendation? For translation quality, no: reviewers across all three directories describe the output as the most accurate and context-aware machine translation they have used, and an August 2026 review says the updated version is noticeably better when translating from English. For support-dependent buyers, yes. If your workflow cannot absorb a week of unanswered email, buy month to month rather than annually, and keep the cancellation path open.

## FAQ

**Can I try DeepL Pro before paying?** Yes for Individual and Team plans. Nothing is charged until the trial ends, and you can cancel during it.

**Can I get a second trial later?** No. DeepL excludes anyone who has previously received a DeepL trial, on any account.

**Can I trial the Write Pro add-on separately?** Only while your main Pro subscription is itself in trial.

**Can I get a refund?** DeepL's FAQ documents cancellation, not refunds. Cancelling stops the next charge at the end of the current cycle; money already paid for the running period is not returned, which is the argument for starting monthly.

**How do I change plans?** Upgrades apply immediately with pro-rata credit for what you paid. Downgrades and cancellations take effect at the end of the billing period.

**Which payment methods work?** Cards and SEPA Direct Debit (EUR accounts) are standard. Bank transfer by virtual IBAN is limited to Team, Business and Enterprise annual subscriptions above a country-specific invoice threshold. PayPal is not accepted.

**How many languages does it cover?** DeepL does not publish a count on the pages reviewed here. Capterra's listing names 24, including the major European languages plus Chinese, Japanese and Traditional Chinese; some reviewers still say coverage is short for the languages they work in.

## Before you check out

Match the plan to the job, not to the tier ladder: text and the odd document means Starter at €8.99/month; several glossaries and a real document load means Advanced; two seats or more means Team for the shared quota; anything involving an API, a CAT tool plug-in or your own software means an API subscription and no Pro plan at all.

Two things to confirm on the live pricing page before you enter card details: the annual price in your own currency, since the 16% figure and the directory listings do not fully agree, and whether the tier you picked includes the glossary count you need. Start monthly if either answer is unclear, then upgrade; the pro-rata credit means switching up later costs you nothing extra.
